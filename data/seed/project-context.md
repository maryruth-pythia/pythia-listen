# Demo project — Composite Hawaii utility-scale solar+storage

> **This is a composite project, not a real proposal.** It is constructed for
> the Pythia Listen demo to give the AI a concrete, plausible site to discuss
> with participants. The community concerns the AI is aware of are sampled
> from real public comments filed in PUC Docket 2022-0250 (Energy Equity
> Docket) — see `docket-2022-0250-comments.json`. The project itself is not.

---

## Project name

Honua Solar & Storage (composite)

## Location

A composite agricultural-zoned site on the leeward side of Hawai'i Island
(Big Island), drawn from typical Stage 3 RFP sites in the area.

## Project type

Utility-scale solar photovoltaic with co-located battery energy storage system
(BESS).

## Project size

40 MW solar + 160 MWh battery storage. Roughly 200 acres on agricultural-zoned
land. Comparable in scale to recent Stage 3 RFP awards on Hawai'i Island.

## Status

Pre-permit. Site control under negotiation. Hawaiian Electric Stage 3 RFP
final award group (December 2023). Power Purchase Agreement under negotiation
with the developer.

## Community Benefits Package

Per Hawaiian Electric's Stage 3 RFP requirement, the developer commits to a
Community Benefits Package of at least $3,000 per megawatt per year of the
contract term — approximately $120,000 per year for this project, distributed
to verified local residents and community-priority programs.

## Public-process posture

- Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) required; scoping notice expected
  within 12 months.
- Public comment period under the EIS will reach property-tax-record-listed
  residents via mail; a public website and at least one community meeting are
  planned.
- Hawaiian Electric's "Aloha protocol" applies to community meetings on
  Hawai'i Island.

## What is being decided right now

The developer is negotiating site control and finalising the PPA. Decisions
that will be shaped, locked, or contested in the next 6–18 months include:

- Specific site footprint and access road routing
- Tariff structure and the form of the Community Benefits Package
- Timing of construction and any phased buildout
- Channels and forms of ongoing engagement with the surrounding community
- Whether and how 'Olelo Hawai'i and other languages are used in materials

## What Pythia is asking participants

Pythia is having short, voluntary conversations with people in or connected
to the surrounding community to understand:

- How they have experienced past energy or land-use decisions in the area
- What is working and what is missing in how communities are currently
  consulted on proposed clean-energy projects
- What — if anything — would make this kind of process feel respectful
- Where their interests overlap with what the developer needs to succeed,
  and where they diverge

## What Pythia is NOT doing

- Pythia is not the project developer.
- Pythia does not represent Hawaiian Electric, the PUC, or any government.
- Pythia is not collecting data to use against the participant.
- Pythia is not promising that participant input will change the project. We
  promise only that what is shared (with explicit permission) will be
  considered and shared back.

## Honest framing of the structural tension

Pythia's commercial customer is the project developer or its financiers. The
beneficiary is the community. We hold this tension honestly — the AI does
not advocate for the developer or for any particular outcome. It listens.
